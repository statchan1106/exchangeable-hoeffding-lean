\section{Purpose and verification contract}

This blueprint connects the proof of
\emph{A Sharper Hoeffding Bound for Weighted Sums of Exchangeable Random
Variables} to named Lean declarations.  Seongchan Lee leads the formalization
of the joint work with his advisor and coauthor Ilmun Kim.

The public entry point is
\[
  \texttt{import Testlean.ExchangeableHoeffding.All}.
\]
The presentation follows mathematical roles and theorem names.  It does not
depend on the physical placement of declarations.

We distinguish two statuses throughout.
\begin{itemize}
  \item A \emph{closed proof} has no project-specific analytic hypothesis and
  contains no placeholder.
  \item A \emph{conditional reduction} is a kernel-checked implication whose
  remaining paper-level statement is visible as a field of a named input
  structure.
\end{itemize}

The target MGF inequality is
\[
  \E \exp\left\{\lambda\sum_{i=1}^{n}
    w_i(X_i-\overline X_N)\right\}
  \le
  \exp\left\{\frac{\lambda^2}{2}\Gamma_N
    \|P_{\one^\perp}\widetilde w\|_2^2\right\}.
\]

\section{Exchangeability and the finite-population factor}

\begin{definition}[Probability model]
\lean{ExchangeableHoeffding.permute,
ExchangeableHoeffding.Exchangeable,
ExchangeableHoeffding.BoundedByOne}
Exchangeability means invariance of the law under every coordinate
permutation.  Boundedness means that all coordinates lie in \([-1,1]\) almost
surely.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Permutation invariance interface]
\lean{ExchangeableHoeffding.exchangeability_supports_symmetrization}
The exchangeability predicate specializes directly to any chosen coordinate
permutation.  The later averaging over all permutations remains part of the
main symmetrization stage.
\end{lemma}

\begin{definition}[Inflation factor]
\label{def:gamma}
\lean{ExchangeableHoeffding.gammaTerm,
ExchangeableHoeffding.Gamma,
ExchangeableHoeffding.GammaClosed,
ExchangeableHoeffding.harmonic,
ExchangeableHoeffding.epsilonBarber}
The finite-population factor is
\[
  \Gamma_N=\max_{1\le s\le\lfloor N/2\rfloor}
  \frac{N(N-s)}s\sum_{\ell=N-s}^{N-1}\frac1{\ell^2}.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Complete analysis of \(\Gamma_N\)]
\label{thm:gamma-complete}
\lean{ExchangeableHoeffding.Gamma_eq_closed,
ExchangeableHoeffding.gamma_asymptotic_expansion,
ExchangeableHoeffding.gamma_strictly_improves_barber,
ExchangeableHoeffding.gammaAnalyticInputs}
The candidate sequence is shown to attain its maximum at
\(s=\lfloor N/2\rfloor\).  Lean then proves the parity-split closed form,
the expansion
\[
  \Gamma_N=1+\frac{3}{2N}+O(N^{-2}),
\]
and the strict comparison with Barber's factor for \(N\ge3\), together with
the endpoint equality at \(N=2\).  The compatibility structure
\texttt{GammaAnalyticInputs} is now closed by the constructor
\texttt{gammaAnalyticInputs}; it is not an outstanding assumption.
\end{theorem}

The formal asymptotic proof uses explicit midpoint-rule error bounds before
passing to the eventual \(O(N^{-2})\) statement.  Thus the finite formula and
the asymptotic statement are connected inside Lean rather than recorded as
independent claims.

\section{Hypergeometric sampling without replacement}

\begin{definition}[Mass, sampling MGF, and quadratic factor]
\lean{ExchangeableHoeffding.hypergeomPMF,
ExchangeableHoeffding.samplingMgf,
ExchangeableHoeffding.hypergeomMgf,
ExchangeableHoeffding.hypergeomB}
For a population of size \(N\), \(K\) success coordinates, and sample size
\(m\), the canonical centered count has mass
\[
  \frac{\binom Kr\binom{N-K}{m-r}}{\binom Nm}.
\]
The finite-sampling quadratic factor is
\[
  B_{N,m}=(N-s)^2\sum_{\ell=N-s}^{N-1}\ell^{-2},
  \qquad s=\min(m,N-m).
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Normalization and the explicit PMF formula]
\lean{ExchangeableHoeffding.sum_hypergeomPMF,
ExchangeableHoeffding.hypergeomMgf_eq_pmf_sum}
Vandermonde's identity proves that the mass sums to one.  A finite partition
of all subsets by their intersection size identifies the subset-sampling MGF
with the explicit hypergeometric mass sum.
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[One-step recursion]
\label{lem:sampling-recursion}
\lean{ExchangeableHoeffding.bounded_centered_mgf,
ExchangeableHoeffding.samplingMgf_step}
The sum over all \(m\)-subsets is decomposed by its first selected element.
The resulting centered increment has conditional mean zero and a deterministic
range.  Hoeffding's lemma controls that increment, producing the exact
martingale recursion for sampling without replacement.
\end{lemma}

\begin{theorem}[Sharpened hypergeometric MGF]
\label{thm:hypergeom}
\lean{ExchangeableHoeffding.samplingMgf_le_smallSamplingB,
ExchangeableHoeffding.samplingMgf_complement,
ExchangeableHoeffding.hypergeometric_mgf_bound,
ExchangeableHoeffding.hypergeometricAnalyticInputs}
\uses{lem:sampling-recursion}
Induction on the recursion gives the small-sample estimate.  Complementing a
sample treats the other half of the range and yields
\[
  \log\E\exp\{t(H-mK/N)\}\le\frac{t^2}{8}B_{N,m}.
\]
The structure \texttt{HypergeometricAnalyticInputs} is now closed by
\texttt{hypergeometricAnalyticInputs}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{lemma}[Transpose symmetry]
\label{lem:hypergeom-symmetry}
\lean{ExchangeableHoeffding.hypergeomPMF_symm,
ExchangeableHoeffding.hypergeomMgf_symm}
On their common support, interchanging \(K\) and \(m\) leaves the mass
unchanged.  Vanishing terms outside the smaller support are handled explicitly,
giving
\[
  \psi_{N,K,m}(t)=\psi_{N,m,K}(t).
\]
This symmetry is essential because the squared norm of a two-level vector is
controlled by its multiplicity, not by the Hamming-slice size.
\end{lemma}

\section{Two-level vectors on a Hamming slice}

\begin{definition}[Slice functional and sphere section]
\lean{ExchangeableHoeffding.sliceSubsets,
ExchangeableHoeffding.sliceAverage,
ExchangeableHoeffding.elemSym,
ExchangeableHoeffding.sliceFunctional,
ExchangeableHoeffding.AtMostTwoValues,
ExchangeableHoeffding.SphereSection}
For a uniform \(k\)-subset \(S\),
\[
  F_{N,k}(y)=\log\E_S\exp\left\{\sum_{i\in S}y_i\right\}
\]
is represented by a normalized elementary symmetric polynomial.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Exact slice formulas]
\lean{ExchangeableHoeffding.sliceSubsets_eq_powersetCard,
ExchangeableHoeffding.card_sliceSubsets,
ExchangeableHoeffding.sliceAverage_one,
ExchangeableHoeffding.sliceAverage_exp_sum_eq_elemSym,
ExchangeableHoeffding.sliceFunctional_eq_log_sliceAverage}
The slice is the fixed-cardinality powerset, has size \(\binom Nk\), and its
exponential average agrees exactly with \(F_{N,k}\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[Canonical two-level representation]
\label{lem:two-level-representation}
\lean{ExchangeableHoeffding.centeredTwoLevelVector,
ExchangeableHoeffding.sum_centeredTwoLevelVector,
ExchangeableHoeffding.normSq_centeredTwoLevelVector,
ExchangeableHoeffding.sum_centeredTwoLevelVector_on,
ExchangeableHoeffding.eq_centeredTwoLevelVector_of_atMostTwoValues}
Every zero-sum vector with at most two coordinate values is represented as
\[
  y_i=\begin{cases}
    \dfrac{N-m}{N}d,&i\in A,\\[4pt]
    -\dfrac mN d,&i\notin A,
  \end{cases}
\]
including the degenerate one-level cases.  Lean proves
\[
  \|y\|_2^2=\frac{m(N-m)}N d^2,
  \qquad
  \sum_{i\in S}y_i=d\left(|S\cap A|-\frac{|S|m}{N}\right).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[Exact reduction to the hypergeometric MGF]
\label{lem:slice-hypergeom}
\lean{ExchangeableHoeffding.samplingMgf_indicator_eq_hypergeomMgf,
ExchangeableHoeffding.sliceAverage_exp_centeredTwoLevelVector_eq_hypergeomMgf}
The MGF of the centered two-level slice sum is exactly the hypergeometric MGF,
not merely bounded by it.
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[Matching \(B_{N,m}\), \(\Gamma_N\), and the norm]
\label{lem:factor-match}
\lean{ExchangeableHoeffding.gammaTerm_le_Gamma,
ExchangeableHoeffding.hypergeomB_eq_gammaTerm_mul_normFactor,
ExchangeableHoeffding.hypergeomB_le_Gamma_mul_normFactor}
Writing \(s=\min(m,N-m)\), Lean proves the exact identity
\[
  B_{N,m}
  =\texttt{gammaTerm}(N,s)\frac{m(N-m)}N
  \le \Gamma_N\frac{m(N-m)}N.
\]
This closes the constant bookkeeping in the paper.
\end{lemma}

\begin{theorem}[Complete two-level slice bound]
\label{thm:two-level-slice}
\lean{ExchangeableHoeffding.log_sliceAverage_centeredTwoLevelVector_le,
ExchangeableHoeffding.slice_mgf_bound_of_atMostTwoValues}
\uses{thm:hypergeom, lem:hypergeom-symmetry,
lem:two-level-representation, lem:slice-hypergeom, lem:factor-match}
For every zero-sum vector with at most two values,
\[
  F_{N,k}(y)\le\frac{\Gamma_N}{8}\|y\|_2^2.
\]
The proof treats empty and full level sets separately, so no hidden
nondegeneracy assumption remains.
\end{theorem}

\section{The remaining extremizer theorem}

\begin{definition}[Three-coordinate input]
\lean{ExchangeableHoeffding.threeObjective,
ExchangeableHoeffding.ThreeSphereSection,
ExchangeableHoeffding.HasDuplicateCoordinate,
ExchangeableHoeffding.ThreePointAnalyticInputs}
For fixed sum and squared norm, the local replacement argument studies
\[
  A\sum_{i=1}^3e^{x_i}+B\sum_{i=1}^3e^{-x_i}.
\]
The input structure records two analytic statements: the fifth-derivative
Hermite-interpolation sign and the conclusion that a maximizer has a repeated
coordinate.  The reciprocal Lagrange tangent identities are already closed as
\texttt{lagrange\_tangent\_sum\_zero} and
\texttt{lagrange\_tangent\_weighted\_sum\_zero}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Global slice extremizer input]
\lean{ExchangeableHoeffding.SliceAnalyticInputs}
The structure now contains only the assertion that, on each zero-sum sphere
section, a maximizer of \(F_{N,k}\) exists with at most two coordinate values.
The hypergeometric estimate is no longer an input field.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Extremizer-to-slice assembly]
\lean{ExchangeableHoeffding.slice_mgf_bound_from_two_level_extremizer,
ExchangeableHoeffding.SliceAnalyticInputs.slice_mgf_bound}
\uses{thm:two-level-slice}
Given only the two-level extremizer assertion, Lean derives the full slice MGF
bound.  The zero-norm sphere is handled directly; for positive norm, the
extremizer comparison and Theorem~\ref{thm:two-level-slice} finish the proof.
Thus the remaining slice-level gap is precisely the global extremizer theorem.
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[Chernoff tail bound]
\lean{ExchangeableHoeffding.integrable_exp_mul_dot_of_bounded,

  \|P_{\one^\perp}\widetilde w\|_2

\begin{proposition}[Universal lower bound]
\lean{ExchangeableHoeffding.AdmissibleConstant,

  C\ge\begin{cases}

  \end{cases}

Its current export is conditional on the corresponding field of

\end{proposition}

\section{Completion ledger}

\subsection{Independent audit of the supplied closure candidate}

An independently supplied closure candidate proposes constructors for the
three-point, slice, and main analytic inputs. Its proof architecture is
mathematically useful: repeated Rolle and Hermite interpolation feed an exact
three-coordinate constraint curve; three-coordinate replacement feeds the
global slice extremizer; and permutation vertices, orbit measures, and a
second-order argument feed the main upper and lower bounds.

The candidate is not marked as closed. In an isolated copy of the verified
development, its proposed closed import fails elaboration in the first analytic
stage. In particular, the repeated-Rolle proof attempts to compose inequalities
whose endpoints do not match: the intended order is
\(s_2<b<r_2<s_3\), but the submitted transitivity term inserts the unrelated
inequality \(a<b\). Several Hermite-derivative and constraint-curve goals also
remain unresolved. Consequently the proposed axiom audit cannot import the
closed development and does not run.

Absence of \texttt{sorry}, \texttt{admit}, or a project-local \texttt{axiom}
declaration is therefore treated only as a source scan, never as a proof
certificate. Promotion requires a successful closed build, a runnable axiom
audit for every unconditional export, and a clean build of the public import in
the same commit.

The machine-checked proof already closes the full \(\Gamma_N\) analysis, the
sampling-without-replacement martingale, the sharpened hypergeometric MGF, the
transpose symmetry, and every two-level slice calculation.

The remaining end-to-end obligations are:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item prove the fifth-derivative interpolation sign and the constrained
  three-coordinate maximum in \texttt{ThreePointAnalyticInputs};
  \item lift the three-coordinate replacement to the global two-level
  extremizer in \texttt{SliceAnalyticInputs};
  \item formalize permutation averaging plus convex reduction from the cube to
  Hamming slices; and
  \item construct the exchangeable witness laws and the second-order argument
  for the parity-dependent lower bound.
\end{enumerate}

Only after these obligations are closed should the public MGF, tail, and lower
bound be advertised without project-specific input arguments.
